# Design throughput: how many binders a card returns per hour

Binder design is a funnel. You generate many designs, filter them, and order a handful. So the
number that decides whether a campaign fits in your calendar is designs per hour per card, not
seconds per design at batch 1. This page is that number for all three designers, measured, with
the knobs that move it.

All figures below are on a Wormhole Galaxy chip (`j10glx02`), AICLK 1000 MHz sampled during the
run, against a 512-residue protein target with an 80-residue binder. That target size is what a
real campaign looks like: a class-A GPCR is 350-480 residues, a class-I pMHC around 380 across
three chains.

## The rates

| model | settings | designs/h per card |
|---|---|---|
| PXDesign | `--num_designs 8 --n_step 400` | 79.6 |
| PXDesign | `--num_designs 1 --n_step 400` | 54.9 |
| RFdiffusion3 | `--num_timesteps 100` | 20.9 |

Numbers come from `perf/mgxscale/` and are regenerated by
`python3 perf/mgxscale/fan.py --plan perf/mgxscale/plans/prod512.txt --out results.jsonl`.

## Generating more than one design at a time

**PXDesign: raise `--num_designs`.** It is the batch axis, so all the designs come from one
batched trajectory and the per-design cost falls: 65.6 s at 1, 54.4 s at 4, 45.2 s at 8. That is
1.45x at batch 8 against one at a time. The designs are genuinely independent — pairwise 9.2 to
24.7 A apart in the target's frame — and geometry does not degrade with batch size.

**RFdiffusion3: `--batch_size` stops helping at this size, and the runtime knows.** Batching
pays below about 3000 atoms and is pinned to 1 above it. A 512-residue target with an
80-residue binder is 4609 atoms, so `--num_designs 4 --batch_size 4` runs four designs one at a
time and takes four times as long. Use `--devices` instead.

**BoltzGen: `--num_designs` is a pipeline-level count**, defaulting to 10000, with `--budget`
setting how many survive filtering (default 30).

## Scaling across cards

Every designer fans data-parallel across cards with `--devices 0,1,2,3`, one pinned process per
card. There is no tensor parallelism and none is needed: designs are independent, so a card
added is throughput added, up to what the host can feed.

```bash
# 32 designs across four cards
tt-bio design specs.json --model rfd3 --from_pdb --num_designs 32 --devices 0,1,2,3
```

## Sizing a campaign

At 79.6 designs/h, BoltzGen's default 10000-design run costs 126 card-hours — 5.2 card-days, or
under 5 hours on a 27-card Galaxy. At RFdiffusion3's 20.9/h the same count is 479 card-hours,
about 18 hours on 27 cards. Both are well inside a design window measured in weeks, which means
the practical limit on a campaign is how many designs you want to filter, not how long the
hardware takes to make them.
