# Design throughput: how many binders a card returns per hour

Binder design is a funnel. You generate many designs, filter them, and order a handful. So the
number that decides whether a campaign fits in your calendar is designs per hour per card, not
seconds per design at batch 1. This page is that number for all three designers, measured, with
the knobs that move it.

All figures below are on a Wormhole Galaxy chip (`j10glx02`), AICLK 1000 MHz sampled during the
run, against a 512-residue protein target with an 80-residue binder. That target size is what a
real campaign looks like: a class-A GPCR is 350-480 residues, a class-I pMHC around 380 across
three chains.

## The rates

| model | settings | designs/h per card |
|---|---|---|
| PXDesign | `--num_designs 32 --n_step 400` | 84.3 |
| PXDesign | `--num_designs 16 --n_step 400` | 83.3 |
| PXDesign | `--num_designs 8 --n_step 400` | 79.6 |
| PXDesign | `--num_designs 1 --n_step 400` | 54.9 |
| BoltzGen | `--num_designs 8 --steps design` | 31.2 |
| BoltzGen | `--num_designs 8`, full pipeline | 13.6 |
| RFdiffusion3 | `--num_designs 4 --num_timesteps 100` | 29.9 |
| RFdiffusion3 | `--num_designs 1 --num_timesteps 100` | 20.9 |

BoltzGen's two rows are the generation step alone and the complete pipeline — generation,
inverse folding, refolding, analysis and filtering. The full pipeline costs 2.3x the
generation step and is what you want if you are choosing binders to order: on that run the
four designs `--budget` kept scored design-to-target ipTM 0.92 to 0.94.

Numbers come from `perf/mgxscale/` and are regenerated by
`python3 perf/mgxscale/fan.py --plan perf/mgxscale/plans/prod512.txt --out results.jsonl`.

## Generating more than one design at a time

**PXDesign: raise `--num_designs`.** It is the batch axis, so all the designs come from one
batched trajectory and the per-design cost falls. Against the 512-residue target: 65.6 s at 1,
54.4 s at 4, 45.2 s at 8, 43.2 s at 16, 42.7 s at 32 — 1.54x at the top, flattening from 16
rather than turning back.

**The gain shrinks as the target grows.** Against a 256-residue target the same curve reads
70.7 s at 1, 26.3 s at 8, 24.1 s at 16: **2.7x** where 512 gives 1.5x. Batching amortises a
fixed per-invocation cost, and on a larger target the per-step device work dominates it. Pick
the batch for the target you have, and there is no penalty for asking for more.

The designs are genuinely independent — pairwise 9.2 to 24.7 A apart in the target's frame at
batch 8 — and geometry does not degrade with batch size. Every geometry flag any of the three
designers raises at a batch reproduces at batch 1 on the same target.

**RFdiffusion3: `--batch_size` stops helping at this size, and the runtime knows.** Batching
pays below about 3000 atoms and is pinned to 1 above it. A 512-residue target with an
80-residue binder is 4609 atoms, so `--num_designs 4 --batch_size 4` runs the four designs one
at a time. Asking for several in one invocation still pays, just for a different reason: the
checkpoint load is amortised, and per-design cost falls from 172.3 s to 120.5 s. Past that,
`--devices` is the lever.

**BoltzGen: `--num_designs` is a pipeline-level count**, defaulting to 10000, with `--budget`
setting how many survive filtering (default 30). It is not a device batch, so asking for more
in one invocation saves only the checkpoint load: 121.8 s per design at 1 against 115.3 s at 8.
`--devices` is the lever.

## Scaling across cards

Every designer fans data-parallel across cards with `--devices 0,1,2,3`, one pinned process per
card. There is no tensor parallelism and none is needed: designs are independent, so a card
added is throughput added.

That is measured, not assumed. Four identical PXDesign jobs started together on four cards of
one Galaxy returned 43.9, 43.9, 44.0 and 44.0 s per design against 45.2 s for the same job
alone — 4.12x aggregate, 0.4 % spread across the cards, on a host that was already busy with
other work.

```bash
# 32 designs across four cards
tt-bio design specs.json --model rfd3 --from_pdb --num_designs 32 --devices 0,1,2,3
```

## Sizing a campaign

BoltzGen's default is 10000 designs. Through the complete pipeline that is 734 card-hours,
about 27 hours on a 27-card Galaxy; generation alone is 320 card-hours. The same count through
PXDesign is 120 card-hours, under 5 hours. Five targets at the full default, full pipeline,
is under six days of one Galaxy. The practical limit on a campaign is how many designs you
want to filter, not how long the hardware takes to make them.
