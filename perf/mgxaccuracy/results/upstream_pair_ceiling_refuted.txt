THE UPSTREAM 1536 PAIR'S ASSUMED COST CEILING IS REFUTED BY OBSERVATION
=========================================================================

2026-09-24 21:38Z. `ps -o time` on the two qb2 draws, differenced against the 20:32Z reading:

    draw a  pid 2095799   219,214 -> 231,908 CPU-s over 3,960 s   =  3.205 cores
    draw b  pid 2767286   151,553 -> 164,061 CPU-s over 3,960 s   =  3.159 cores

Neither has written a refold cif. qb2's load is unchanged at 32.6 on 16 cores against 32.5 an
hour ago.

TWO THINGS FOLLOW, ONE OF THEM UNCOMFORTABLE.

1. THE PHASE DIAGNOSIS IS CONFIRMED. At 20:32Z both draws had halved from ~2.25 cores to ~1.15
   while qb2's load FELL, and `results/pass_notes.txt` called that a single-threaded phase
   rather than contention. They have now both recovered to ~3.2 cores, together, with the load
   flat. Contention does not do that either. Two independent jobs moving in lockstep through
   1.15 -> 3.2 cores while the host stays still is the jobs' own pipeline, and the diagnostic
   in pass_notes stands.

2. THE COST CEILING IS GONE. The row has been carrying a band of (204.4k, 229.2k] CPU-s for a
   draw, raising the floor by observation each pass and leaving the ceiling where an early
   estimate put it. **Draw a is now at 231,908 CPU-s and still running**, which is past 229.2k.
   The ceiling was never measured — it was an extrapolation — and it is now falsified. There is
   no evidence-backed upper bound on this draw's cost, so there is no honest ETA to quote for
   it, and every ETA this row has published for the qb2 pair (~22:00Z, then ~22:02Z, then
   ~22:57Z) came off that ceiling and was wrong.

WHAT TO SAY INSTEAD. Draw a has cost > 231.9k CPU-s and is running at 3.2 cores; draw b has
cost > 164.1k and is running at 3.2 cores, about 68k CPU-s behind a. The only defensible
statement about when they land is that a leads b by roughly 5.9 h of CPU at the current rate.
Report the floor and the rate, not a finish time, until one of them writes a refold cif — which
is the completion test either way (`ref/harvest_upstream_1536.sh`).

THE GENERAL SHAPE. A band with a measured floor and an estimated ceiling reads like a
measurement and is half of one. Every pass tightened the floor and none of them ever tested the
ceiling, so the ceiling survived on nobody having looked at it — the same structure as
`a-stale-ceiling-justifies-not-looking`. Raising a floor is not narrowing a band unless the
ceiling is measured too.

WHY IT FAILED: THE EXPONENT, NOT THE THREAD CAP
================================================

Corrected after checking the ceiling's own derivation instead of calling it "an extrapolation"
and leaving it there. `results/upstream_pair_eta.txt` builds it from three things:

    the 512 valid draw's design step ran 5126.3 s at OMP_NUM_THREADS=6
    its average core occupancy c was never recorded and is not recoverable
    a 1536 draw costs 5126.3 x c x (1616/592)^2 = 38,198 x c CPU-s
    UPPER: c <= 6.0, the thread ceiling  ->  229,187 CPU-s

Two assumptions, not one. The thread cap c <= 6 is sound and is not what broke: both draws have
been observed at 1.15, 2.25 and 3.2 cores, never near 6. What broke is the **exponent of 2**.
Draw a has now spent 231,908 CPU-s and is still running, so:

    if the 512 draw ran at   observed ratio is at least   implied exponent at least
        c = 6.000 cores                7.54x                      2.012
        c = 4.775 (the banked floor)   9.47x                      2.239
        c = 3.000                     15.08x                      2.702
        c = 2.250                     20.11x                      2.988

Even at the most generous c the exponent is already past 2, and at the occupancies actually
observed on these draws it is past 2.7. Upstream's CPU cost grows FASTER than the square of the
token count, and the ceiling assumed exactly the square.

THE DEVICE GOES THE OTHER WAY, which is why the row never caught this from its own numbers. The
same boltzgen harness on GroEL: 1842.0 s at 592 tokens, 5416.0 at 1104, 11842.0 at 1616, so

    592 -> 1104 tokens   2.940x   exponent 1.731
    592 -> 1616 tokens   6.429x   exponent 1.853

sub-quadratic at both steps. A cost model fitted on the device and carried across to a CPU
reference is a cost model fitted on the wrong machine; the row has a memory for exactly this
shape (`a-reference-is-part-of-the-measurements-identity`) and did not apply it to a WALL-CLOCK
prediction, only to quality numbers.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT CHANGE. No quality number, and not the pre-registered reading of the pair.
It changes only what can be said about when they finish: nothing, until a refold cif appears.
