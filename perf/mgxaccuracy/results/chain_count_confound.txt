GroEL's chains are ~524 residues, so ANY crop of this target above ~524 residues necessarily
adds a second chain. The size ladder and the chain count are therefore not separable on this
target, and the separation is perfect: the only two cells without a genuine second chain are
the only two cells that refold.

THE ROW HAS ALREADY SEPARATED THEM ONCE, ON THE OTHER TARGET, AND THE CARRIER WAS THE CHAIN
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

`results/gpb_supporting.txt`, banked before this pass: 1GPB's chains are ~1008 residues, long
enough to hold a big single-chain crop, and the 512 -> 1536 size effect decomposes into

    +3.33 A   for 823 residues of ONE chain          does not reject
   +14.77 A   for adding chain B at the same crop    rejects

So on the target where the two CAN be separated, adding a chain carried the effect and stretching
one chain to 823 residues did not. That is one target and one decomposition, and "does not
reject" at n=8 is not "is zero" — but it points the opposite way from a residue-count rule.

WHAT THIS CHANGES AND WHAT IT DOES NOT
---------------------------------------

DOES NOT CHANGE: every number in QUALITY. The cells are what they are and the ladder still says
a user who crops GroEL to 512 gets designs that refold and one who crops to 1024 or 1536 does
not. The practical instruction is unaffected for anyone cropping GroEL.

DOES CHANGE what the instruction is ABOUT. "Crop to about 512 residues" is a residue-count rule
read off a ladder that cannot distinguish residue count from chain count, and the one experiment
that did distinguish them chose the chain. A user with a 700-residue SINGLE-CHAIN target is told
by the residue rule to crop away 200 residues, on no evidence that applies to their case, while
a user with two 300-residue chains is told they are fine when the chain rule would warn them.

WHAT THE 768 RUNG DOES AND DOES NOT SETTLE. Both 768 crops carry a second partial chain (244 and
280 residues), so 768 adds residues AND chain content over 512 and cannot separate them either.
It remains a valid CEILING measurement, which is what `plans/groel_768.txt` claims for it, and
it is not a test of this confound. Do not read it as one.

THE CONTROL THAT WOULD SETTLE IT CANNOT BE BUILT ON EITHER TARGET THIS ROW HAS.

Corrected within this pass, after writing the paragraph above and then checking the noun rather
than the number. The obvious control -- a bigger single-chain rung on 1GPB -- does not exist:
`results/size_within_one_chain.txt` states that chain A of the 1GPB dimer IS 823 residues, so
the 823 cell is already ALL of it and there is no 1000-residue single-chain 1GPB crop to run.
GroEL's chains are ~524. Neither target has a chain long enough to put a single-chain cell at
1024 or 1536, which is why `size_within_one_chain.txt` says in its own words that isolating the
interface "would need a >=1536-residue single chain, which this protein does not have".

So the control needs a THIRD target: one protein with a single chain of 1024-1536 residues,
cropped at 512 / 1024 / 1536 with the same 80-residue binder and the same n=8, which holds chain
count at one across the whole ladder. That is a new target with its own fixture work, not
another rung on an existing one. Not launched this pass, and not something the 768 rung or the
qb2 reference pair can substitute for.

WHAT WAS ALREADY KNOWN AND WHAT IS NEW. `results/size_within_one_chain.txt` identified this
confound on 1GPB and measured the decomposition; its first line is "ADDING 311 RESIDUES WITHIN
ONE CHAIN DOES NOT MEASURABLY HURT. ADDING A SECOND CHAIN DOES." What is new here is that the
same confound is perfect on GROEL, the target the row's verdict rests on and whose fixtures
nobody had censused, and that the row's user-facing instruction and VERDICT were still worded
as a residue rule while the only decomposition available pointed at the chain.

So on 1GPB too, every 512 cell is a single chain and every 1536 cell is two. Put beside the
GroEL table above, this is the row's whole size axis:

  EVERY cell measured at 512 is one chain, or one chain plus a stub of 24 residues.

The one point anywhere in the row that separates residue count from chain count is the
823-residue single-chain 1GPB cell, and it moved the median 4.631 -> 7.962 A without rejecting
at n=8 while the chain move at the same crop cost 14.77 A and did reject. One point is one
point. But the row does not have a second, and the size axis it has published is, on the
evidence of the fixtures themselves, a chain-count axis.


THE GROUPING, AND WHY IT IS NOT EXTRA EVIDENCE
-----------------------------------------------

Counting only chain fragments of >= 100 residues (so GroEL's 24- and 12-residue stubs do not
count as chains), the six banked GroEL medians group without overlap:

    1 chain    0.852   1.739     <- 5 of 8 and 7 of 8 under 4 A
    2 chains   8.718  14.457     <- 0 of 8
    3 chains  15.606  14.387     <- 0 of 8

and the 1 -> 2 step is far larger than the 2 -> 3 step. That is a tidy picture and it is NOT
additional evidence for anything. Size and chain count are perfectly confounded in these six
cells, so every monotone function of size orders them exactly the same way; this table is the
same numbers already in `results/groel_2x2_result.txt` and `results/groel_1024_result.txt`,
re-sorted. It is written down because it makes the prediction the FAS control has to beat
concrete -- if chain count is the carrier, FAS at 1536 on one chain should sit with the top row
and not the bottom one -- and for no other reason.

THE WITHIN-TARGET CHAIN TEST IS NOT BUILDABLE WITH A CONTIGUOUS CROP, AND WAS NOT LAUNCHED
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

2026-09-24 21:3xZ, with two cards free and 15 minutes of pass left, the obvious next experiment
was a TWO-chain 768 crop of the SAME protein: FAS single-chain 768 against FAS two-chain 768
holds target, size and binder fixed and moves only chain count, which is the clean test this
whole file is about. It was built, checked, and NOT launched, because it does not pass the
row's own validity criterion.

`fas_dimer_3966.cif` (chains A+B of 2VZ8) is a real complex: min inter-chain atom distance
2.31 A, 25,539 inter-chain edges under 22 A, ONE connected component over all 3966 residues,
Rg 61.6 A. The whole assembly is fine. The 768-residue CROPS are not:

    offset 1500   A 462, B 306    2 components [462, 306]    Rg 48.2 A
    offset 1600   A 362, B 406    2 components [406, 362]    Rg 48.3 A
    offset 1700   A 262, B 506    2 components [506, 262]    Rg 49.7 A

Every contiguous window across the sequence boundary is DISCONNECTED under the 2-22 A graph.
The A/B interface of this dimer is not at the sequence boundary, so a contiguous crop that
contains residues from both chains does not contain the contact between them. A disconnected
conditioning graph is what disqualified `perf/bhdesign/targets/big_1831.cif` and what got this
row's first 1536 reference answer WITHDRAWN
(`results/upstream_reference_wrong_fixture.txt`); running one here would have manufactured
exactly the artefact the row already paid for once.

WHAT WOULD BE NEEDED: a crop selected by interface proximity rather than by a contiguous
`--crop-offset` window, which is a fixture construction neither `job.py` nor
`make_contact_target.py` currently does. That is a real piece of work, not a rung, and it
should be scoped rather than improvised. Until it exists, the chain-vs-residues question is
answered only by the single-chain FAS ladder, where a 1536 cell is still the pre-registered
decision.
