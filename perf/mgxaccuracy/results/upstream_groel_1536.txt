## THE RANKS, WHICH ARE THE POINT

At 512 upstream is WORSE than seven of the device's eight, beating only the 9.694 outlier.
At 1536 upstream is BETTER than seven of eight, beaten only by the 11.313 minimum.
Exact one-sided p at m=1 against n=8 is (U+1)/9, so 0.222 in each direction: **neither rung
orders the two sides, and a single draw cannot.** That floor is why the 1GPB gate needed two
(results/upstream_draw_count.txt).

The direction flip is worth naming rather than smoothing over, because it is the unflattering
reading and it is the first thing in this row that has pointed at the port at all: relative to
one upstream draw the device goes from clearly better at 512 to slightly worse at 1536. Two
single draws with p=0.222 each cannot support that, and the deciding 1GPB pair -- m=2, the
pre-registered test, three device cells -- reads U = 5 / 4 / 11 of 16 and finds no ordering
either (results/upstream_1536_pair_result.txt). So the flip is recorded and not claimed.

## WHAT IT DOES SETTLE, WITHOUT ANY TEST

Upstream at 1536 returns 12.842 A against a 4 A permissive bar. Upstream fails here too, on its
own defaults at fp32 on CPU, on the target whose 512 cell this row's best designs came from. A
size at which the reference itself stops producing a refoldable binder is a property of
BoltzGen, and that is the sentence the brief's clause 4 asks for.
