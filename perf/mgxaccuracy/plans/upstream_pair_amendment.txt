## 1. DRAW A CAN FORECLOSE CLAUSE 3 ON ITS OWN. THE BAR IS THE SECOND-SMALLEST DEVICE VALUE.

The correction in `results/upstream_pair_rule_correction.txt` says clause 3 opens on more than
perfect separation: at m=2 against n=8 the exact one-sided p is 1/45 = 0.0222 when both upstream
draws sit below all eight device designs (U=16), and 2/45 = 0.0444 when exactly one device design
slips below the larger draw (U=15). Both are <= 0.05. U=14 gives 3/45 = 0.0889 and does not.

So clause 3 needs U >= 15, and U >= 15 tolerates exactly ONE device value below an upstream draw.
If draw a alone already exceeds TWO of a cell's eight, then draw a contributes at most 6 to U, draw
b at most 8, and U <= 14 whatever draw b turns out to be. The cell is foreclosed on draw a alone.

The threshold is therefore the cell's SECOND-SMALLEST design, not its minimum. Verified by feeding
`read_upstream_pair.exact_p_less` a draw b of -1e9, i.e. the best draw b that could possibly exist:

    cell                          min      2nd-smallest    draw a > 2nd-smallest => best-case p
    PRIMARY   after cc908c377     6.650      18.740        U=14, p=0.0889, FORECLOSED
    onetree   72ad980ee          17.843      18.808        U=14, p=0.0889, FORECLOSED
    pre-fix   d8_s400_gpb        19.311      20.140        U=14, p=0.0889, FORECLOSED

  If draw a > 20.140 A, all three cells are foreclosed and the pair's verdict is clause 4 —
  upstream is at the same level — before draw b lands.

This is arithmetic on the pre-registered test, not a new test. It does NOT let the row close early:
`closing_rule.txt` §4(a) requires the PAIR to have landed and been read, and that still governs.
What it licenses is the opposite of haste — the next pass may state the scientific verdict once
draw a is in, and must still wait for draw b before flipping the row, so the wait costs nothing
and the claim is not held hostage to a draw that is 6-10 h behind. If draw a lands under 20.140
on any cell, nothing is foreclosed there and draw b decides it; say so and wait.

## 2. WHICH CELL DECIDES, AND WHAT A DISAGREEMENT MEANS.

`read_upstream_pair.py` names PRIMARY the deciding cell because the row's published 1536 number
rests on it, and prints the other two because "which tree a device cell came from" is the confound
this row already controlled once. Its docstring flags that the three minima disagree — 6.650 /
17.843 / 19.311, a spread of 12.66 A — and PRIMARY's 6.650 is a lone outlier against its own other
seven (18.740 to 22.924). Taken at face value that is an alarming way to decide a verdict: one
lucky device design out of 24 would set the bar that protects the port from a clause-3 finding,
which is the flattering direction and exactly the kind of reading this row has withdrawn before.

It is not what happens, because §1 above is the operative test and the second-smallest values are
18.740 / 18.808 / 20.140 — a spread of 1.40 A, not 12.66. The outlier costs PRIMARY one crossing
and the test already grants one. The three cells agree about where the decision sits.

Pre-registered anyway, since the 1.40 A gap is not zero and a draw can land inside it:

  ALL THREE AGREE      read the verdict off PRIMARY as the script already does.

  THEY DISAGREE        i.e. draw a (or the pair) falls between 18.740 and 20.140, so PRIMARY
  (report clause 3)    and/or onetree foreclose while pre-fix does not, or the reverse.
                       Then the row does NOT close on PRIMARY alone. Report the disagreement with
                       all three p-values and treat clause 3 as OPEN — the conservative reading,
                       and the unflattering one. A verdict that depends on which of three
                       byte-identical-fixture cells you pick is not a verdict; it is a reason to
                       go locate the defect, which is what clause 3 asks for.

## 3. ONE LABEL FIX IN THE TOOL, NO BEHAVIOUR CHANGE.

`read_upstream_pair.py` prints `separation: PERFECT/no` from `up[1] < min(dev)`, which is the U=16
test, while its verdict line correctly uses p <= 0.05, which also fires at U=15. A reader can
therefore see "separation: no" beside "upstream BETTER at 0.05" and think the tool contradicts
itself. Print U and the crossing count instead. The decision is unchanged; only the label moves.
