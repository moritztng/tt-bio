## The defect, as precisely as it is currently known

Norm ratio **0.907813**, cosine **0.998430** — so the squared error is **74.88 % magnitude and
25.12 % direction**. **Our single track is 9.22 % too small in norm and points almost exactly the
right way.** That is a magnitude deficit, not a wrong transform.

It does **not accumulate — it cliffs**. Depth ladder, our port and upstream 0.4.3 float64 from
the same captured block-0 input, 19 rungs:

    k        1      8      16     24     32     42     44     45     46     47     48
    s     8.2e-3 5.3e-3 7.1e-3 7.5e-3 1.1e-2 1.5e-2 1.4e-2 1.7e-2 5.4e-2 9.9e-2 1.07e-1
    r     0.9923 0.9982 0.9978 0.9960 0.9906 0.9867 0.9873 0.9920 0.9576 0.9113 0.9078

**Forty-five blocks produce 1.7e-02 and the last three produce the rest — 6.22x in 3 of 48.**
Do not fit a growth exponent to this; the row that found it explicitly declined to, because a
cliff is not a power law and the fitted values (0.398 coarse, 5.435 fine) describe neither half.

**The control that says whose cliff it is.** Upstream's own bf16-autocast arm over the same rungs
also finds the last blocks harder — 3.6x over that stretch — but **ours grows 7.4x**, and the
ratio to upstream's own bf16 goes 6.16x, 7.82x, **10.09x at k=45, then 24.15x at k=46**. One more
block multiplies our excess by 2.39x.

**The one mechanical clue.** The reference's own scale turns over exactly there: `||s||` grows
1.55e6 -> 2.18e6 across the last four blocks while `||z||` falls 1.95e5 -> 8.80e4. And **nothing
in the port is block-index-dependent** — `T.Pairformer` builds all 48 `PairformerLayer`s with
identical arguments — so this is a **data-dependent precision loss in the regime where the two
tracks trade magnitude**, not a per-block bug.

## What to do

   inferring from the ladder. Block 46 is the sharpest single step (10.09x -> 24.15x); instrument

2. **MECHANISM** — name the operation that loses the 9.22 %, with the dynamic range beside it.
   The obvious hypothesis, which you must test rather than assume: a fixed-scale bf16 step whose
